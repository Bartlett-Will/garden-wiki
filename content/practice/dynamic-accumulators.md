# Dynamic Accumulators — Kelp, Comfrey, and Alfalfa

> **Quick take:** Kelp, comfrey, and alfalfa mine nutrients no ordinary garden plant can reach — deep subsoil minerals or the open ocean — and concentrate them into biomass you can use as nearly free fertility.

[[clackamas-coot|Clackamas Coot]] calls kelp, comfrey, and alfalfa the top "bio-nutrient accumulators." Each accesses a nutrient pool unavailable to your crops, used alone or in combination they "do nothing but good things" for soil. They anchor the amendment list in [[the-coot-mix]].

## Kelp

A plant that grows 200–250 feet a year **without a root system**, accumulating **83 elements** from seawater — and crucially, in the right balance, because it excludes what would harm its own growth (notably salt). Humans have harvested North Atlantic kelp as fertilizer since 1372.

Buying notes:
- **Kelp meal** (the dried, ground form) preserves what matters. Coot's objection to processed **powders/liquids** (the sodium-hydroxide extraction behind products like Maxicrop, originally an agricultural product from Norway): the plant hormones and secondary metabolites are destroyed
- **Alginic acid** — kelp's standout compound — holds 250–300% of its weight in water, a meaningful moisture-retention boost for raised beds and outdoor containers
- The balanced 83 elements stand in contrast to single-mineral thinking. High-dose magnesium "corrections" are a classic way to ruin soil — magnesium tightens soil the way milk of magnesia tightens your gut, restricting air and water flow and skewing cation exchange

## Comfrey

A deep-rooted perennial (roots run about twice as deep as a fruit tree's) that pulls subsoil minerals inaccessible to shallower-rooted crops and converts them into huge amounts of leaf biomass — roughly 99% water and rich in nitrate, so it disappears quickly when used as mulch.

**Use the Bocking 14 sterile cultivar.** The naming: in the 1930s, nurseryman Lawrence Hills (on Bocking Road, Essex, England) trialed ~20 numbered comfrey strains — research that included a station in Canby, Oregon. Bocking 14 is the survivor; every plant today is a root cutting lineage from a single plant established around 1945.

Cautions and practice:
- **Placement is a lifetime commitment.** Comfrey owns its ground; removal in the Portland area starts at $300 per plant, and rototilling it multiplies it — a quarter-inch of root starts a new plant
- **Skip the stinky tea.** Coot abandoned soaking comfrey into liquid feed: just chop and drop as green mulch and let it vanish into the soil
- Multiple cuttings per season once established (first cutting can come weeks early in a wet year); meaningful biomass from year two
- Watch for powdery mildew on fall foliage — compost it rather than mulching it into the garden

## Alfalfa

Grown mostly east of the Cascades in some of the world's richest volcanic soils, alfalfa is a multi-year stand whose roots reach **30+ feet** by year four or five — nearly twice comfrey's depth. The harvested meal carries those deep-mined minerals. (Caution from [[vermicomposting]]: keep alfalfa meal under ~5% in worm bedding — excess nitrate kills worms.)

## Using Accumulators in Compost

Timing matters: don't add accumulators at the start of a hot compost pile — thermophilic temperatures burn up exactly the compounds you're adding them for. Add them in the **mesophilic stage**, once the pile has dropped below ~100°F and begun its months-long cure. As with cold-processed castings, the secondary metabolites then remain intact (see [[composting-and-organic-inputs]] on cure time).

> [!cannabis] Cannabis Application
> Kelp meal, alfalfa meal, and chop-and-dropped comfrey cover trace mineral and nitrogen needs in living-soil cannabis without bottled products — Coot's prescription for a struggling soil is simply "kelp, alfalfa, and neem." Use kelp *meal* in the mix and as top-dress; alginic acid's water retention is especially useful in fabric pots, which dry quickly. If you plant comfrey near outdoor beds, remember it is permanent — site it as a border, not in the bed.

## Key Takeaways

- Accumulators access nutrient pools your crops can't: ocean water (kelp) and deep subsoil (comfrey, alfalfa)
- Kelp accumulates 83 elements in balance and excludes salt; alginic acid holds 250–300% of its weight in water
- Prefer kelp meal over processed powders/liquids, which lose hormones and metabolites
- Plant only sterile Bocking 14 comfrey, and only where it can stay forever
- Chop-and-drop beats comfrey tea
- Add accumulators to compost in the mesophilic (cooling) stage, never the hot stage
- Beware single-mineral oversupply — excess magnesium wrecks soil structure
