# Botanical Teas

> **Quick take:** Aerate a cup or so of plant meal — alfalfa, kelp, neem — in a bucket of water overnight, water it in, and throw the spent muck in the worm bin: a complete liquid feeding program from three bags of amendments.

Botanical teas are water extractions of plant meals. Unlike [[compost-tea]], which is brewed to multiply *microbes*, botanical teas extract *soluble nutrients, hormones, and saponins* directly from the material. They're [[clackamas-coot|Clackamas Coot]]'s answer to bottled nutrients — he spent years visiting struggling gardens, and the first thing he'd do was brew an alfalfa-kelp tea: "There was never a garden that didn't see vast improvement within 48 hours, period."

## The Core Recipe: Alfalfa-Kelp Tea

Per 5 gallons of water:

- **1 cup alfalfa meal**
- **¼ cup kelp meal**
- Aerate (or just stir periodically) **overnight**
- Strain, and water it in

That's the whole program. Expect visible response within about 48 hours.

## Why Alfalfa Foams: Saponins

Alfalfa meal contains roughly **30,000 ppm saponins** — the natural surfactants that make a bubbling alfalfa tea foam dramatically. For comparison, yucca extract (the premium-priced surfactant product) is about 40,000 ppm, and **soap nuts** are around 250,000 ppm (25%). Saponin research in plant health goes back 30+ years; in Asia, pressed tea-seed meal serves the same role. Practical upshots:

- Alfalfa tea brings its own wetting agent — no yucca purchase needed
- Soap nut tea is strong enough to emulsify neem oil for foliar sprays (alfalfa alone is not) — see [[neem-and-karanja]]

## Neem Meal Tea for Fungus Gnats

Aerate or stir **2 cups neem meal in 5 gallons for 24 hours**, then drench the soil. The residual oil (meals retain 7–9% after pressing) and active compounds kill fungus gnat larvae. This works as both an IPM treatment and a general soil soak.

## The Combination Brew

Coot's full version mixes all three — **kelp, neem, and alfalfa**, scaled down proportionally — bubbled for about **18 hours**, then applied.

## Nothing Is Wasted: the Worm Bin Loop

A tea only extracts about **45–50%** of what's in a meal. The spent muck goes straight into the [[vermicomposting|worm bin]], where the worms recover the rest. Two uses from every input: a liquid feed now, castings later. This is the same closed-loop thinking as [[reusing-soil]].

> [!cannabis] Cannabis Application
> Botanical teas are the standard "transition path" for cannabis growers coming from bottled nutrient lines — they keep the familiar ritual of mixing a liquid feed while moving the actual fertility program into the soil. An alfalfa-kelp tea during veg and the neem drench at the first sign of fungus gnats cover the two most common indoor issues without anything that shows up on a COA.

## Key Takeaways

- Alfalfa-kelp tea: 1 cup alfalfa + ¼ cup kelp per 5 gallons, aerated overnight — visible results within 48 hours
- Botanical teas extract nutrients; compost tea grows microbes — different tools
- Alfalfa's ~30,000 ppm saponins make it self-surfacting; soap nuts (250,000 ppm) emulsify neem oil
- Neem meal tea (2 cups/5 gal, 24 h) drenches out fungus gnat larvae
- Teas pull only 45–50% of the goodness — compost the muck in the worm bin
