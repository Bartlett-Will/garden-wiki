# Hop Latent Viroid

> **Quick take:** Hop latent viroid (HLVd) is the pathogen behind "dudding" in cannabis — stunted, low-potency plants — but because it is *latent*, healthy unstressed plants can carry it without showing symptoms, which reshapes how a living-soil grower manages it.

Hop latent viroid is a tiny infectious RNA agent now widespread in commercial cannabis. It causes **dudding** — plants that grow stunted, lose vigor, and produce dramatically reduced yield and cannabinoid content. Growers have historically called affected plants "duds" or, at Green Life Productions, "shiners" (after the *pure sunshine* strain that expressed it badly). Because the viroid is latent, infection and symptom expression are two different things — a distinction that drives the entire management strategy.

This page captures the practical, in-the-field perspective of [[steve-cantwell|Steve Cantwell]], a commercial living-soil grower. It is grower experience, not a clinical protocol; the podcast notes that a dedicated HLVd episode with Dr. Punja's research was forthcoming.

## Latent Means Expression Is Manageable

The key insight is that a plant can carry HLVd without becoming a dud, *if* it is kept healthy and unstressed. Two independent observations from the episode support this:

- Cantwell stopped seeing dudding once he **culled the chronically affected genetics** (his *pure sunshine* line). He no longer sees dudding plants in his living organic system, even leaving roots in the soil.
- A colleague (Justin at Mako Farms) has tested positive for HLVd yet, by keeping plants healthy and stress-free, does not see the dudding expression and still pulls large yields.

The takeaway: reducing plant stress suppresses viroid expression. Coming in with clean plants helps, but even beds that previously held infected root systems can produce big healthy crops if the plants are kept unstressed. This connects directly to [[plant-stress-responses]] and to good cultural practice generally — [[field-observation|observation]], [[watering-and-the-air-water-relationship|correct watering]], and a healthy [[soil-food-web|soil biology]].

## Genetics and Susceptibility

Some genetics carry and express viral loads more readily than others; some are more susceptible. Cantwell's ideal would be a facility of genetics that *tolerate* the viroid rather than being sterilized of it. Research (referenced to Dr. Punja) supports that certain genetics express HLVd less than others. Culling a strain that reliably duds 50–60% of a bed was, for him, simpler than fighting it.

## Tissue Culture: Effective but Often Overkill

100% tissue-culture propagation can produce virus-free starts, and some state-of-the-art facilities rely on it entirely. Cantwell considers this overkill for many operations — he notes touring spotless tissue-culture setups whose flowering rooms nonetheless looked "like a powdered donut factory" from powdery mildew. Clean propagation does not substitute for setting the stage against *other* pathogens.

## Roots, Heavy Metals, and the Living-Soil Tension

There is a genuine tension in living-soil systems:

- **The case for pulling roots ([[tad-hussey|Tad Hussey]]):** HLVd viral load is higher in root tissue than leaf tissue, so removing root balls lowers the viral load left in the bed. Pulling roots also removes accumulated **heavy metals** via biomass. This argues for harvesting root balls.
- **The case for leaving roots (Cantwell):** Harvesting root balls strips out the worms, bacteria, and fungi nesting there and feeding on [[photosynthesis-and-root-exudates|root exudates]] — "one step forward, two steps back," to the point his worm populations nearly collapsed. His solution is a **grid-planting pattern**: plant in one spot this cycle, the adjacent spot next cycle, letting old root balls break down in place while the biology recovers. At harvest the plant is lifted and the soil shaken off, so root hairs stay in the bed.

Both views are internally consistent; the choice depends on whether a grower prioritizes viral/heavy-metal load reduction or soil-biology continuity.

## Setting the Stage

Cantwell frames disease suppression as "setting the stage" — maintaining a healthy aerobic, diverse, beneficial-organism-rich environment so pathogens like HLVd, botrytis, and powdery mildew have no opening. Good air circulation, filtration, healthy aerobic soil, quality compost, metered watering, and following [[the-5s-system|SOPs]] all stack the deck. "Willy-nilly" hand watering and chasing social-media techniques set the stage for the opposite.

> [!cannabis] Cannabis Application
> HLVd is a cannabis-specific crisis — it is in most facilities and there is no cure. The pragmatic living-soil stance is: start with clean genetics where possible, cull strains that reliably dud, keep plants unstressed so a latent infection stays latent, and decide deliberately whether to pull or leave root balls. Aggressive sterilization (100% tissue culture, throwing out living soil) is one valid path, but keeping a tolerant, healthy, low-stress crop is another — and it preserves the living-soil methodology Cantwell is unwilling to abandon over the viroid.

## Key Takeaways

- HLVd causes dudding — stunted, low-potency cannabis — but is *latent*, so healthy unstressed plants can carry it without expressing symptoms
- Reducing plant stress is the central management lever; clean starts and tolerant genetics help
- Culling chronically affected genetics can solve a persistent dudding problem outright
- 100% tissue culture works but is often overkill and doesn't address other pathogens
- Viral load and heavy metals concentrate in roots — pulling root balls lowers them but harms soil biology; grid planting is a middle path
- "Set the stage": aerobic, diverse, well-managed soil and airflow suppress the viroid's expression
