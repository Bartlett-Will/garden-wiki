# Autoflowering Cannabis

> **Quick take:** Autoflowering cannabis flowers on a timer instead of a light schedule — seed to harvest in 7–9 weeks, any time of year — which is why [[jeff-lowenfels]] predicts it will become "the next tomato" of home gardening.

Most cannabis varieties (the indica- and sativa-derived lines behind nearly all modern strains) are **photoperiod plants**: they begin flowering only when nights grow long enough. Autoflowering varieties don't care. They flower based on age, not light cycle, which collapses the growing calendar and removes the single biggest scheduling constraint in cannabis cultivation. [[jeff-lowenfels]] considers them significant enough that he wrote his fourth book about them — *DIY Autoflowering Cannabis: A New Way to Grow* — aimed not at professional growers but at ordinary gardeners.

## Where They Come From

Autoflowering genetics trace to *Cannabis ruderalis*, first documented around 1900–1903 by a scientist in the Volga River region of Russia (populations were later found in Siberia, Canada, and reportedly along Alaskan railroad tracks). It's a scrubby plant, roughly a foot to a foot and a half tall, with essentially no THC — which is why it was ignored for most of a century.

Its photoperiod independence is a clean example of evolution. These were escapees from hemp plantations (grown for rope and sailcloth) at high latitudes, where waiting for long autumn nights means dying before setting seed. Plants that flowered on age rather than day length survived; the rest didn't.

Breeders crossed ruderalis with modern photoperiod lines, and after generations of selection, today's autoflowers are — in Jeff's words — almost as strong and almost as productive as regular cannabis, while keeping the ruderalis trait.

## Why They Matter

- **Speed**: 7–9 weeks from seed to harvest, versus months for photoperiod varieties.
- **Any season**: grow indoors or out whenever temperatures allow; no light-deprivation tarps, no timer discipline, no worrying about a porch light interrupting the dark cycle.
- **Breeding velocity**: a 7–9 week generation instead of 7–9 months means breeding programs (including home breeding) iterate roughly four times faster. Crossing two autoflowers is straightforward; crossing an autoflower with a photoperiod plant works too but takes more generations to stabilize the trait.
- **Accessibility**: the speed and simplicity make them a realistic first plant for gardeners who would never run a light-cycle grow. Jeff's comparison is the snap pea — a genuinely new plant category that went from nonexistent to ubiquitous within a generation of gardeners.

## Growing Notes

Because an autoflower spends only ~9 weeks in the ground, it behaves like a fast annual and clearly favors **bacterially dominated soil** — Jeff reports this consistently across his own testing. Two cautions from his experience:

- Bacterially dominated does not mean fungus-free. All functioning soil needs fungal activity for decomposition and structure — see [[soil-food-web]].
- **Don't make the soil too rich.** Autoflowers do poorly in heavily amended, hot mixes. Their short life leaves no time to recover from overfeeding.

The general crop-duration rule from [[ep-050-jeff-lowenfels-returns|episode 50]]: plants in the ground less than a year lean bacterial; more than a year, lean fungal. Autoflowers sit at the extreme bacterial end of that spectrum. Longer-season photoperiod varieties (e.g., 9-month Afghani lines) want proportionally more fungal presence — see [[mycorrhizal-fungi]].

There is no transplant-timing flexibility to lean on: with such a short cycle, stress setbacks (rootbound starts, transplant shock, overwatering) cost a meaningful fraction of the plant's whole life. Get [[watering-and-the-air-water-relationship|watering]] right and start them in their final container when possible.

## Key Takeaways

- Autoflowers descend from *Cannabis ruderalis*, which evolved age-based flowering after escaping high-latitude hemp plantations
- No photoperiod requirement: grow any time of year, indoors or out
- Seed to harvest in 7–9 weeks; potency and yield now approach photoperiod strains
- Short-cycle plants favor bacterially dominated (but never fungus-free) soil, and dislike overly rich mixes
- Generation time ~4× faster than photoperiod cannabis, which accelerates breeding dramatically
- Jeff Lowenfels' book: *DIY Autoflowering Cannabis: A New Way to Grow*
