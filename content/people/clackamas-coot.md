# Clackamas Coot (Jim Bennett)

> **Quick take:** The grower who gave the living-soil community its most famous free soil recipe — the Coot Mix — and introduced malted barley as a soil amendment to the cannabis world.

Jim Bennett, known online as Clackamas Coot (on forums like ICMag and as Lumper Dogs on Grasscity), is an organic grower and former nursery owner from the Portland, Oregon area. He is best known for his Neem, Crab, and Kelp soil mix — the "[[the-coot-mix|Coot Mix]]" — which he shares freely, and for pioneering [[malted-barley-and-enzyme-teas|sprouted seed enzyme teas]] made from malted barley. He sells nothing and is openly disdainful of bottled, proprietary nutrient products: "All I ever did was pass out information about how people could make the soil better."

He was an early mentor and friend to podcast host [[tad-hussey]], who credits Coot with significantly shaping the KIS Organics soil mixes and nutrient packs.

## Key Contributions to the Wiki

- **[[the-coot-mix]]** — the equal-thirds living soil recipe (peat, aeration, castings) with kelp, neem, and crab meal, derived from the 1930s Cornell mix
- **[[malted-barley-and-enzyme-teas]]** — bringing diastatic malted grains and their enzyme suite (chitinase, phosphatase, protease, urease, amylase) into horticulture
- **[[vermicomposting]]** — neem meal and malted barley as worm-bin accelerants; cold-process castings that preserve secondary metabolites
- **[[dynamic-accumulators]]** — advocacy for kelp, comfrey, and alfalfa as the core "bio-nutrient accumulator" amendments, including the history of Bocking 14 comfrey
- **[[neem-and-karanja]]** — whole-input neem advocacy: sourcing quality meal, the 2:1 neem-karanja foliar blend, and neem's soil-first Indian tradition
- **[[kelp-and-seaweed]]** — the kelp meal vs. seaweed extract distinction and the T.L. Senn research lineage
- **[[botanical-teas]]** — the alfalfa-kelp tea ("never a garden that didn't see vast improvement within 48 hours") and the spent-muck-to-worm-bin loop
- **[[sphagnum-peat-and-potting-soils]]** — insider knowledge of the CSPMA harvest system and the commercial potting soil loss-leader economy

## Background

- Owned a nursery growing field plants like Japanese laceleaf maples — the source of his preference for pumice over perlite (perlite floats to the surface; pumice stays in the strata)
- Trained six months at the San Francisco Baking Institute plus three weeks in French bakeries; 20+ years of bread baking led directly to his malted barley discovery (diastatic malt used in lean doughs)
- Has grown the same cannabis plant through mother cuttings for over 33 years, giving him a genetic control for evaluating any change in practice
- Collaborates with Doug at Northwest Red Worms on windrow vermicompost production and casting trials

## Approach

Coot's philosophy is radical input minimalism backed by biology: quality worm castings you make yourself, a handful of plant- and ocean-derived amendments chosen for completeness (kelp's 83 balanced elements), basalt rock dust, and malted grain enzymes. "If you use bad biology in your soil, you're going to have a bad soil... garbage in, garbage out." He is skeptical of nutrient marketing ("ammonium nitrate is ammonium nitrate"), product mythology ("proprietary" blends), and growers who modify his recipe with poor-quality components and then blame the recipe.
