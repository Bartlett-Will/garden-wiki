# Rob Knechtel

> **Quick take:** Founder of Black Swallow Living Soils — the Canadian living-soil supplier who turned 40 years of manufacturing and logistics experience into a coast-to-coast organic amendment business, and co-developed the Canadian KISS Mix with Tad Hussey.

Rob Knechtel founded Black Swallow Living Soils in Brantford, southern Ontario, around 2015 with his son Nathan (who runs day-to-day operations) and his wife Lou, anticipating Canadian cannabis legalization. Before that, Rob spent over 40 years in a completely unrelated industry — designing, installing, and OEM-manufacturing fireplaces — which gave him the global logistics experience that became Black Swallow's quiet advantage: knowing how to move products across borders and across Canada.

His personal interest in growing cannabis dates to the late 1960s, but he is frank that decades of personal growing experience left him "miles away" from current soil science — building Black Swallow meant extensive reading, coursework, and trial and error.

## Black Swallow Living Soils

A respected Canadian supplier of organic soil amendments and growing supplies, shipping coast to coast:

- Blends the **KISS Mix** (based on Tad Hussey's formulation) and offers a **nutrient pack** — the mineral portion of the mix — so growers can source bulky inputs (peat, aeration, compost) locally rather than paying soil freight across Canada
- Canadian distributor for Fertile Pots (biodegradable pots); also works with Grassroots fabric pots and stocks Blue Mat irrigation supplies
- Pioneered **prilled amendments** (lignin binder) for dust-free, consistent handling; as of this episode, Rob and Tad were developing a prilled grow-and-bloom fertilizer for both the US and Canada

## Perspective

Rob's recurring theme is patience and observation: hold your feet on the ground, start with water only, change one thing at a time, and accept that "you never reach the finish line" in gardening. His hardest daily challenge: helping growers from hydroponic backgrounds let go of bottle-feeding assumptions that don't apply to living soil.
