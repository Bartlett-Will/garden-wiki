# Bryant Mason

> **Quick take:** Certified crop advisor known as "the Soil Doctor" who combines precision laboratory soil testing with data science to manage organic cannabis nutrition, especially in soilless media.

Bryant Mason is a certified crop advisor through the American Association of Agronomy. He runs Soil Doctor Consulting (soildoctorconsulting.com, Instagram: @soil_doctor), specializing in organic nutrient management for cannabis grown in soilless media, and also advises outdoor cultivators across the U.S. on nutrient management and cover cropping. At the time of [[ep-072-heavy-metals-cover-crops-and-water-ph|episode 72]] he had spent several years combining precision lab techniques with data science to unravel cannabis soil and crop nutrition, reviewing thousands of cannabis soil tests.

## Key Contributions

- **Chemistry-first management philosophy** — long-term microbial community composition is dictated by physical and chemical conditions (especially pH), so optimize those and let the biology fall into place. Measurable-but-not-manageable biological tests lack targets ([[soil-ph-and-nutrient-availability]]).
- **Soilless media reinterpretation** — classical topsoil soil science (CEC, organic matter numbers, sampling assumptions) must be reinterpreted in clay-free, fast-draining container media; think solubility, not colloid balancing ([[soilless-media-vs-topsoil]]).
- **Sampling pragmatism** — across thousands of tests, sample-to-sample variability rarely changes the management decision; growers commonly oversample. Uses Logan Labs exclusively for target consistency, and recommends sending irrigation water for saturated paste tests ([[soil-testing-and-diagnosis]]).
- **Nitrogen and potassium management** — N and K (plus calcium) are removed in the highest quantities; targets ~75 ppm nitrate going into flower, drawing down to 20-30 ppm by harvest; advocates soluble amino acid nitrogen to spare the ~18% of photosynthetic energy spent on nitrate reduction ([[nitrogen-and-phosphorus]]).
- **Compost evaluation framework** — ammonium:nitrate ratio, C:N ratio (~18:1 rule), sodium/salinity, and total NPK as the quality screen; compost as the slow-release "bank account" for N and K ([[composting-and-organic-inputs]]).
- **Water chemistry teaching** — alkalinity and bicarbonates matter more than the water's pH reading; acidify above ~120 ppm total alkalinity ([[water-chemistry-and-alkalinity]]).
- **Heavy metals input database** — sourced heavy metal tests across input categories (kelp, soft rock phosphate, alfalfa, poultry, neem, gypsum, rock dusts); kelp meal is the first place to look for arsenic failures ([[heavy-metals-management]]).
- **Evidence-driven humility on cover crops** — cites the 2020 meta-analysis showing blends matched single species 88% of the time; favors simple, goal-driven cover cropping outdoors and cautions against cover crops in commercial indoor production ([[cover-crops-and-crop-rotation]]).
- **Soil-test literacy for containers** — teaches that CEC and organic matter on a peat-media test are misleading (CEC is a calculated sum of cations, not a colloid measurement; OM via loss-on-ignition just weighs peat/coir carbon and tracks bulk density), and that exchangeable hydrogen is a redundant restatement of pH ([[soilless-media-vs-topsoil]]).
- **Sufficiency-then-balance method** — achieve sufficient fertility first, then balance the cations off the saturated-paste percentages (~Ca 50 / Mg 20 / K 20 / 10% other) rather than chasing standard-test BCSR ratios; treats sodium as the worst excess and magnesium as the trickiest to manage in peat ([[soil-testing-and-diagnosis]]).
- **Phosphorus realism** — phosphorus is the least soil-test-predictable nutrient (biologically mineralized; low soluble paste P is normal), so manage it off tissue tests; Mulder's-chart P antagonisms barely show in cannabis ([[nitrogen-and-phosphorus]]).
- **Evolved amino-acid stance** — revised his earlier view: doubts plants uptake amino acids directly in quantity (microbes convert them to nitrate first), so values amino-acid amendments for particle size and fast availability, not a metabolic shortcut ([[nitrogen-and-phosphorus]]).
- **Tooling: the Soil Doctor tool and course** — built a "digital agronomist" that converts a soil test plus grow parameters (indoor/outdoor, cycle length, soil volume, compost feedstocks, preferred N sources) into a precision organic amendment recommendation, and the 12+ hour *Become a Cannabis Soil Expert* course; both embody "test, don't guess" and recommending only what's needed.
