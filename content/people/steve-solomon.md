# Steve Solomon

> **Quick take:** The gardener-author who discovered the hard way that compost alone isn't enough — his *Intelligent Gardener* made soil-test-driven remineralization accessible to home growers.

Steve Solomon is the author of *The Intelligent Gardener* and *Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades* (seven editions), and the founder of Territorial Seed Company, which he started in Oregon with $13,000 and built around an honest seed-trials ground. He later moved to Tasmania, Australia, where he continues to garden and advise on soil testing — including for a licensed hemp operation.

## Background

Solomon began as a true believer in the compost-solves-everything school of organic gardening ("all that Rodale bullshit," in his words). Living almost entirely off his own Oregon trials garden, he and his wife Isabel Moser — a naturopath — both developed deteriorating health: loose teeth, brittle hair and nails, fading energy.

A six-month stay in Suva, Fiji reversed everything. The market vegetables there grew in the Singatoga Valley on silt from ultra-basic igneous rock — naturally remineralized every few years by floods — with zero fertilizer for 75 years. The realization that mineral content, not organic purity, determined food quality redirected his life's work toward remineralization, building on Weston Price's nutrition documentation and William Albrecht's soil chemistry.

## Intellectual Style

Solomon is openly self-revising — each edition of *Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades* is "a totally different book," and he warns readers that his older assertions may be wrong. He is also a demanding skeptic ("I'm from Missouri, man — you've got to show me"), notably about rock dust claims, for which he proposes leaf tissue analysis as the decisive test. He flags narrative-driven reasoning in the organics movement while still engaging seriously with microbial nutrient cycling.
