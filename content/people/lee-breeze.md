## Notable Contributions and Claims

Lee Breeze brings a refreshing skepticism to soil biology hype. His core message: **details matter, context is everything, and profitability trumps ideology.**

Key points:

- **"pH is the overriding factor of nutrient availability"** — optimal range is 5.8-6.4, with 6.2 as the sweet spot. Organic systems often naturally buffer into this range (high organic matter provides more exchange sites), which creates the false impression that pH does not matter.
- **Biological soil tests are not yet actionable** — PLFA and CO2 respiration tests are highly sensitive to sampling conditions, we do not yet know which organisms to cultivate, and growers would do the same management regardless of the results. "The best biological indicator is a healthy crop."
- **Humic acids are context-dependent** — dramatic on low-CEC sandy soils (can increase CEC by 10x), but negligible on moderate-to-high CEC soils. "Like taking aspirin when you don't have a headache."
- **Adding sugar to soil is counterproductive** — sugar is intercepted within nanometers of application and disrupts the symbiotic relationship where bacteria trade fixed nitrogen for plant sugars.
- **Cover crops are not universally good** — in arid regions they consume limiting water; indoors they create disease, pest, and nutrient competition risks. Define the specific goal before choosing a cover crop.
- **Tillage is a legitimate tool** when targeted — for disease management, compaction repair, or moisture management. Minimum aggression needed.
- **Profitability over yield** — track actual costs and calculate profit, not just bushels per acre.
