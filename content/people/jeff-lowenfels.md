# Jeff Lowenfels

> **Quick take:** Author of the Teaming trilogy — the books that brought soil food web science to mainstream gardening.

Jeff Lowenfels is a nationally respected garden writer based in Anchorage, Alaska. He is the co-author and author of three foundational books on soil biology for gardeners, often called the "Teaming trilogy":

1. **Teaming with Microbes: The Organic Gardener's Guide to the Soil Food Web** (2006, revised 2011) — Introduced the [[soil-food-web]] concept to home gardeners. Won the Garden Writers of America Gold Award and has been called "the most important gardening book published in the past 25 years."
2. **Teaming with Nutrients: The Organic Gardener's Guide to Optimizing Plant Nutrition** (2013) — Explores how plants actually uptake and use nutrients at the cellular level. Covers the symplast and apoplast transport systems, membrane proteins, and why understanding plant physiology changes how you approach feeding.
3. **Teaming with Fungi** (2017) — Dedicated entirely to [[mycorrhizal-fungi]], their biology, taxonomy, and practical application. Written because knowledge of mycorrhizal fungi had expanded so dramatically since the first book.
4. **Teaming with Bacteria** — His most recent in the series, on the [[rhizophagy-cycle]] and [[endophytic-bacteria]]: how bacteria enter plants, supply nutrients and phytohormones, and fix nitrogen internally. Discussed in [[ep-104-teaming-with-bacteria|episode 104]]. Lowenfels recommends reading the series in the order *Microbes → Bacteria → Fungi → Nutrients*.
5. **DIY Autoflowering Cannabis: A New Way to Grow** — Written for ordinary gardeners rather than professional growers, making the case that [[autoflowering-cannabis]] will be "the next tomato." Previewed in [[ep-050-jeff-lowenfels-returns|episode 50]].

The Teaming books have been translated into 11 languages. Looking back, Jeff names **algae** as his biggest omission from the soil food web books, and he encourages reading the trilogy as a connected whole rather than three standalone books.

## Notable Positions

Lowenfels has evolved his thinking over the years. He originally held a strictly biology-first position (get the microbes right and the soil will provide everything), aligned with [[dr-elaine-ingham]]'s approach. After writing *Teaming with Nutrients*, he broadened to advocate for combining biological management with soil mineral testing — acknowledging that while biology can theoretically unlock any nutrient, it's easier and more reliable when the minerals are actually present. As he puts it: "Nobody ever fertilizes the redwoods... but if you give it the right biology *and* a little biology plant food, things do pretty darn good."

In [[ep-050-jeff-lowenfels-returns|episode 50]] he publicly revised his earlier claim that cannabis simply prefers bacterially dominated soil. His updated position: crop duration decides — in the ground less than a year leans bacterial, more than a year leans fungal — and either way "bacterially dominated" never means fungus-free. He acknowledges [[dr-elaine-ingham]]'s research pointing slightly fungal for cannabis and declines to argue with her "until I have empirical evidence." On pH, his formulation is that it only counts at the rhizosphere — microbes buffer most situations, but extreme inputs still require correction.
