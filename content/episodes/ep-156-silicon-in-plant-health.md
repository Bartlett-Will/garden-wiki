# Episode 156: Silicon in Plant Health

> **Foundations Series #1** | Guest: [[dr-wendy-zellner|Dr. Wendy Zellner]]

## Summary

[[tad-hussey|Tad Hussey]] interviews [[dr-wendy-zellner|Dr. Wendy Zellner]], a plant biologist with 17+ years of silicon research, for a comprehensive look at why silicon belongs in every grower's toolkit. Dr. Zellner explains that silicon does not fight diseases or pests directly — it enables plants to mount their own defense responses at full genetic potential. The conversation covers silicon chemistry (the only plant-available form is silicic acid), transport biology (xylem only, no redistribution once deposited), the heavy metal connection (silicon deficiency increases arsenic uptake), and a thorough myth-busting session on silicon product marketing claims.

## Key Topics

- [[silicon-in-plants]] — silicon is the second most abundant element in soils but the plant-available fraction (silicic acid) is tiny; cannabis is a hyperaccumulator at ~2% tissue concentration
- [[plant-stress-responses]] — silicon enables the plant's own immune and stress responses: water regulation, enzymatic defense reactions, physical cell wall barriers, heavy metal exclusion
- [[silicon-supplementation]] — target 30-60 ppm Si in soilless/hydro systems; all sources equivalent at the same concentration; drench preferred for systemic distribution
- [[soil-testing-and-diagnosis]] — ask vendors for tissue analysis data proving silicon uptake, not just marketing photos

## Key Takeaways

- **Silicon is a nutrient, not a pesticide.** It does not directly fight powdery mildew, botrytis, root rot, or insects. Instead, it enables the plant to express its full genetic potential for defense — enzymatic reactions, physical barriers, and stress signaling all work better with adequate silicon.
- **Cannabis is a silicon hyperaccumulator** — tissue concentration (~2% Si) exceeds calcium, potassium, and even nitrogen. This means cannabis is especially vulnerable when silicon is deficient.
- **Silicic acid is the only plant-available form** — also called orthosilicic acid or monosilicic acid. These are three names for the same molecule. Premium "monosilicic acid" products are chemically identical to standard silicic acid and do not justify higher prices.
- **Silicon moves only through the xylem** (transpiration stream), never the phloem. Once deposited in a leaf, it stays there permanently. Root drench provides systemic protection; foliar spray protects only the tissue it contacts and must be reapplied to new growth.
- **Silicon deficiency triggers accidental heavy metal uptake** — silicon-deficient hyperaccumulators activate extra transport proteins that also absorb arsenic (chemically similar, adjacent on the periodic table).
- **Silicate-mineralizing bacteria** in the rhizosphere weather rock minerals and release silicic acid. Living soil systems get better at silicon release over successive growing cycles.
- **pH is not a concern** for silicon availability — solubility is constant between pH 4 and 9.
- **Label math matters** — SiO2 inflates the apparent silicon content by roughly 2x, and Si(OH)4 inflates by roughly 3.4x. Convert all labels to % Si for honest comparison.
- **Rice hulls are only good silicon sources if the rice was grown with silicon fertilization.** Always test for arsenic contamination.
- **Perlite releases silicon** — growers using perlite-based media may need less supplementation than they think.
