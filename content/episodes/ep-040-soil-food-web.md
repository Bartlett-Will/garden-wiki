# Episode 40: The Soil Food Web

> **Foundations Series #3** | Solo episode by [[tad-hussey|Tad Hussey]]

## Summary

Tad Hussey delivers a solo deep dive into the [[soil-food-web|soil food web]] — what the science actually says, where popular understanding has drifted into dogma, and how to apply biological principles in controlled growing environments without falling for oversimplifications. The episode opens with a tribute to [[dr-elaine-ingham|Dr. Elaine Ingham]], who passed away the week it was recorded and whose work popularized the soil food web concept. Tad then walks through the mechanisms of microbial nutrient cycling, the [[rhizophagy-cycle|rhizophagy cycle]] discovered by [[dr-james-white|Dr. James White]], and the practical question of how to harness biology in a grow room without blindly copying a forest floor.

## Key Topics

- [[soil-food-web]] — the network of organisms exchanging energy and nutrients, driven by photosynthetic carbon
- [[photosynthesis-and-root-exudates]] — plants pump up to 40% of carbohydrates out through roots as exudates to recruit and feed microbes
- [[rhizophagy-cycle]] — plants internalize bacteria, strip their cell walls, absorb nutrients, then eject surviving bacteria through root hair tips
- [[biocontrols-and-ipm]] — Trichoderma, Beauveria bassiana, Metarhizium, and mycorrhizal inoculation as targeted biological tools
- [[composting-and-organic-inputs]] — aerated compost tea as inoculant (not fertilizer), and the contamination risks of raw compost
- [[soil-testing-and-diagnosis]] — why "you never need to soil test" is a dangerous myth; microbes cannot create minerals from nothing
- [[soil-ph-and-nutrient-availability]] — debunking the claim that microbes buffer pH automatically

## Key Takeaways

- The soil food web is real and important, but it has been oversimplified into ideology by some practitioners. Microbes are not alchemists — they cannot create minerals that are not present in the soil.
- The plant is "the only mint in town" — photosynthetic carbon drives the entire soil food web. Exudate composition changes based on what the plant needs, making it a sophisticated solar-powered barter system.
- The [[rhizophagy-cycle]] is a major recent discovery: plants lure bacteria into root tips, attack them with superoxide to strip nutrients, then eject survivors to scavenge again. "The plant isn't trying to kill the bacteria — it's trying to milk them."
- Blindly copying nature does not work for indoor cannabis. Nature does not optimize — it survives. A 4x4 grow tent is an extraction site, not a forest floor.
- Predators (protozoa, nematodes) eating microbes release nutrients in the same ionic forms as synthetic fertilizers — biology delivers them "just in time, directly to the root tip."
- High-carbon amendments can cause microbes to immobilize available nitrogen, starving the plant. What looks like a deficiency may actually be microbial competition.
- Compost carries the highest contamination risk of any input (heavy metals, PFAS, pathogens). Use targeted biostimulants and lab-grown high-CFU strains when possible.
