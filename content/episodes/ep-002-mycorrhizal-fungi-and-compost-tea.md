# Episode 2: Mycorrhizal Fungi, Compost Tea, and the Soil Food Web

> **Quick take:** Jeff Lowenfels walks through his Teaming trilogy, explaining mycorrhizal fungi biology, why compost tea works, the law of return for soil reuse, and how understanding plant cell physiology changes everything about how you garden.

**Guest:** [[jeff-lowenfels]] — author of *Teaming with Microbes*, *Teaming with Nutrients*, and *Teaming with Fungi*
**Host:** [[tad-hussey]]

## Key Topics

### Mycorrhizal Fungi Deep Dive
- 96% of plants form mycorrhizal associations — endomycorrhizal for garden crops and cannabis, ectomycorrhizal for conifers and forest trees
- *Rhizophagus irregularis* is the key species for cannabis and most garden crops
- The plant controls the relationship via chemical signaling — excess P or N prevents colonization
- Inoculate at the seed (roll seeds in inoculant) or at transplant (apply directly to roots)
- Allow 10–14 days minimum before heavy phosphorus application
- Glomalin from mycorrhizal fungi accounts for ~30–35% of soil carbon
- Taxonomy in flux due to new DNA/RNA testing capabilities

### Compost Tea Theory
- Compost tea extracts the "fertilizer bags" (bacteria, fungi) and "fertilizer spreaders" (protozoa, nematodes) from compost
- Feeding during brewing multiplies these populations
- Application as drench or foliar spray delivers biology to the rhizosphere
- Quality of input compost is paramount — homemade or fresh vermicompost beats store-bought
- Indigenous microbes (from your own area/soil) are preferred when possible

### The Law of Return and Soil Reuse
- Nature recycles everything — the redwoods are never fertilized
- Harvesting crops breaks the law of return; amendments put it back
- Old root channels serve as infrastructure for new roots — cut plants at the base, don't pull them
- Established microbial communities, mycorrhizal networks, and soil structure are preserved
- Discarding soil makes no sense in an organic system

### Nutrient Uptake and Plant Physiology
- Every nutrient enters plant cells through specific membrane transport proteins — phosphorus can't use nitrogen's tunnel
- **Symplast pathway:** nutrients move cell-to-cell through cytoplasm, all the way from root to shoot tip
- **Apoplast pathway:** nutrients travel along cell walls without entering cells
- Fulvic acid may use the apoplast pathway; its exact transport mechanism is still debated
- Foliar feeding has limitations — not all nutrients are mobile once inside the plant

### No-Till in Context
- Outdoor no-till prevents soil loss (the original USDA rationale) and protects biology
- Indoor container growing doesn't face erosion risk — light disturbance is acceptable
- Don't pulverize; do incorporate amendments when needed
- Rototilling destroys mycorrhizal networks and glomalin-based carbon storage
