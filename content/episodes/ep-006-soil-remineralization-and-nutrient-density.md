# Episode 6: Soil Remineralization and Nutrient Density

> **Quick take:** Steve Solomon, author of *The Intelligent Gardener*, explains how mineral-depleted soil produces mineral-depleted food — and how soil testing and remineralization restore flavor, disease resistance, and the nutrition that keeps the people eating the food healthy.

**Guest:** [[steve-solomon]] — Author of *The Intelligent Gardener*, founder of Territorial Seed Company
**Host:** [[tad-hussey]]

## Key Topics

### The Fiji Illumination
- Solomon and his wife Isabel ate almost entirely from his Oregon seed-trials garden and both *declined* in health — wobbly teeth, brittle hair, soft fingernails, low energy
- Six months in Suva, Fiji eating market vegetables reversed every symptom
- The vegetables came from the Singatoga Valley: 75 years of European vegetable production with **no fertilizer at all**
- The valley floods every ~3 years, depositing silt from a watershed of ultra-basic igneous rock — freshly ground, highly mineralized rock dust loaded with calcium, magnesium, potassium, iron, zinc, manganese, and copper
- The food was grown with heavy pesticide use, yet still made them healthy — leading to his line: "The problem is not that there's residues of pesticides in our food. The problem is that there's only residues of nutrition left on our foods"

### Weston Price and Nutrition
- Price's *Nutrition and Physical Degeneration* (1930s) documented isolated peoples eating exclusively local food
- Near-perfect dental health across dozens of groups; young people who left for towns returned with mouths full of decay
- Documented cavities that **healed and scarred over with enamel** in well-nourished people
- The isolated groups ingested 2–4× the mineral nutrition of the average 1930s American
- Full nutritional restoration is multigenerational — mothers need several generations of nutrient reserves to build fully healthy babies

### Remineralization Results in the Garden
- Solomon's food has tasted better every year since he began full remineralization
- Subsoil takes years to remineralize as nutrients leach down — deep-feeding crops (carrots, parsnips, beets, chicories, endive) improve last
- His endive stopped developing winter leaf-rot disease entirely and lost most of its bitterness once nutrition reached two feet deep
- Remineralized produce resists mold after harvest — it dries and shrinks rather than rotting

### Base Cation Balancing vs. Sufficiency
- Albrecht worked out the calcium/magnesium/potassium/phosphorus relationships for healthy, high-protein crops; later farm advisors developed **base cation balancing** from his springboard
- Two fertilization philosophies: *sufficiency* (economic rationalist — add only what increases yield profitably) vs. the *ethical* approach (target levels that optimize the health of those eating the crop)
- Modern testing makes this cheap: ~$14 at Spectrum Analytic, ~$25 at Logan Labs; what matters is the **relationship between the numbers**, not small absolute differences between labs

### The Rock Dust Question
- Solomon's skepticism: a ton of basalt dust at 3 ppm zinc contains ~3 g zinc, mostly unavailable; soluble zinc sulfate delivers pounds of available zinc per acre
- Making rock dust deliver meaningful trace elements could take 15–40 tons per acre
- Tad's counter: rock dust feeds microbes that cycle nutrients into plant-available forms over time ([[soil-food-web]])
- Solomon's proposed test: **leaf tissue analysis** — though cannabis can't legally be mailed across state lines for testing
- Connects to silicate-mineralizing bacteria and microbial weathering covered in [[silicon-in-plants]]

### Container Growing and CEC
- A growing medium with no buffering capacity fed by soluble nutrients "is hydroponics, even if it looks like soil"
- With CEC above ~20, nutrients can be mixed into the medium, held on exchange sites, and drawn off by the plant — stable nutrition, fertilized like field soil
- KIS Organics soils run CEC 20–30 with ~100 lbs of mineral amendments per yard, thermally composted two weeks before planting
- A 7-gallon container is roughly the minimum to run water-only through a full cannabis cycle; larger beds grow healthier crops

### Cannabis and Hemp Nutrition
- Hemp/cannabis needs roughly **twice the potassium** of most vegetable crops and draws heavily on manganese
- Use *Intelligent Gardener* targets for everything except potassium (run ~2×) — confirmed independently by Tad's trials
- Nitrogen demand drops to a third or quarter in flower: nitrogen mostly builds chlorophyll, and flowers have little
- Trace element sourcing under organic programs (NOP) is hard — sulfates aren't allowed without documented deficiency; chelated forms (e.g., Biomin) may be OMRI-listed alternatives at half the rate
