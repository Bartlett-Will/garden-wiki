# Episode 5: Compost Tea, Microscopy, and Living Soil

> **Quick take:** Tim Wilson shares his microscope-verified approach to compost tea brewing, his 7-year no-till growing system, superior vermicomposting methods, and the microbial nutrient loop that makes living soil work.

**Guest:** [[tim-wilson]] — Microbe Organics founder, compost tea researcher, microscopist
**Host:** [[tad-hussey]]

## Key Topics

### Living Soil Systems
- Wilson grew cannabis in stacked bins under HPS lights for the BC Compassion Club Society
- Achieved **7 years continuous no-till** in the same soil bins — just top-dressing and inoculating between harvests
- 60-day crop cycle, 18-inch plants, same or better yields than larger plants in longer cycles
- Brought in rove beetles, *Amblyseius cucumeris*, and *Phytoseiulus persimilis* with outdoor soil — they established breeding populations indoors and controlled pests naturally

### The Microbial Nutrient Loop
- Bacteria and archaea process organic matter using organic acids (citric acid, etc.)
- Protozoa (flagellates and naked amoeba) consume bacteria, using only 10–40% of the energy
- The remaining 60–90% is expelled as **plant-available ions** — directly usable by roots
- This is the economic engine of living soil: the plant invests carbon via exudates to feed bacteria, which triggers protozoa to emerge and begin nutrient cycling
- The process mirrors what happens inside a compost tea brewer

### Organic Acids and Nutrient Release
- Both plant roots and soil microbes excrete organic acids (e.g., citric acid with 8 hydrogen bonds)
- These acids liberate cations from clay particles and organic matter via hydrogen exchange
- Example: citric acid's 8 hydrogen bonds can release 4 units of calcium (Ca²⁺) from CEC sites
- This is how nutrients actually become available — not just through passive cation exchange, but through active biological and root-driven acid release

### Vermicomposting
- Two methods: large outdoor heap (horse manure, peat moss, wheat bran, alfalfa meal <5%, kelp meal) and indoor bins with kitchen scraps
- The "neglect" method: let worms redigest their substrate thoroughly over months rather than constantly adding fresh food
- Produces a gummy, dark material visually different from commercial castings
- Consistently superior microbial results under the microscope

### Compost Tea Methodology
- Aerated compost tea = water + aeration + compost + food source
- **36-hour brew time** for peak protozoa activity and nutrient cycling
- Bacteria peak in first 12 hours; protozoa emerge at 24 hours, peak at 36
- Beyond 48 hours: ciliates take over — associated with anaerobic conditions
- Air lifts provide 10x dissolved oxygen vs. air stones
- Simple recipe (vermicompost + molasses) matches complex recipes in microbial results
- Perpetual brewers produce inconsistent product — reconstituting resets the cycle for 24 hours

### Myths Debunked
- **Molasses and E. coli:** Lab studies inoculated with E. coli, then blamed molasses for growth. Any food source would have the same effect
- **Foam = quality:** No consistent correlation under the microscope
- **Refrigeration preserves tea:** Microbes die or go dormant and don't recover
- **Lab testing of shipped samples:** Community changes during transit; results don't reflect brew-time biology

### Soil Mineral Balancing
- Wilson's position: ensure all necessary mineral components are present but don't obsess over exact ratios (Kinsey, Solomon, Reams, etc.)
- The plant works in concert with microorganisms to acquire what it needs — strict mineral "balance" formulas may oversimplify a dynamic biological process
- Peat moss has high CEC and serves as an excellent nutrient reservoir
