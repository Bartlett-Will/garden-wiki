# Episode 104: Teaming with Bacteria

> **Quick take:** Jeff Lowenfels returns to discuss his book *Teaming with Bacteria*, walking through the rhizophagy cycle step by step and introducing endophytic bacteria — internal microbes that feed the plant, make its hormones, fix nitrogen from the air, and may even be behind cannabis trichome production.

**Guest:** [[jeff-lowenfels]] — author of the Teaming series, former president of the Garden Writers of America
**Host:** [[tad-hussey]]

## Key Topics

### The Rhizophagy Cycle, Step by Step
- Builds on the 2006 [[soil-food-web]] model: plants drip [[photosynthesis-and-root-exudates|exudates]] into the rhizosphere to attract bacteria and fungi
- Bacteria are drawn toward the meristem (new growth, very thin cell walls) following a butyric-acid "buttered popcorn" smell
- They enter the **periplasmic space** between the cell wall and the cytoplasm membrane (Lowenfels' tofu-container analogy)
- The plant sprays **superoxide**, stripping the bacterial cell walls into wall-less **L-forms**; the wall material (micronutrients, metals) is absorbed as plant nutrition
- Bacteria produce an antioxidant — **nitrite** — to defend against the superoxide; the nitrite is converted to **nitrate**, which the plant absorbs. Up to ~30% of a plant's nitrogen can come through this cycle
- Multiplying bacteria produce **ethylene**, which makes the meristem cell grow and form a tube — the **root hair** — then are expelled out the root-hair tip back into the soil to regrow their walls and repeat
- Lowenfels' framing: Dr. Ingham's soil food web is a "farming" model; rhizophagy is a "ranching" model (sheep brought in, sheared, returned to pasture)

### Endophytic Bacteria
- Endophytes live inside the plant for part of their lives; can also be fungi, archaea, viruses, micro-eukaryotes
- Multiple entry routes beyond rhizophagy: root-branch cracks, stomata, wounds
- Produce six or seven real phytohormones (auxins, gibberellic acid, ethylene) and several plant nutrients
- Some fix atmospheric nitrogen *internally* — distinct from *Rhizobium* nodules but loosely parallel; "the holy grail of agriculture"
- Strain-specific to the host plant, unlike generalist [[mycorrhizal-fungi]]
- Pass between generations via the **seed** — sterilizing seeds removes beneficial organisms

### Cannabis-Specific Findings
- Endophyte communities differ from cannabis cultivar to cultivar — "the terroir we have been looking for"
- [[dr-james-white]]'s theory: bacteria in glandular trichomes fix nitrogen in a low-oxygen environment created by cannabinoid bombardment — cannabinoids as a byproduct of nitrogen fixation
- Don't sterilize seeds; plant the paper-towel fragment from germination too

### The Minerals vs. Microbes Debate
- Lowenfels and Hussey both place themselves "in the middle" — add minerals (Albrecht-style) *and* steward the microbes
- Bacteria are required for root hairs to form at all (per [[dr-james-white]]); remove them and roots fail to develop properly
- Biostimulants/microbial inoculants are a fast-growing area, but regulation is poor — products may not match their labels; experiment with your own controls

### Compost Tea and Compost
- Vermicompost and thermocompost host genuinely different bacterial families, so they do different things
- Both Lowenfels and Hussey acknowledge compost tea's inherent variability makes it hard to study (echoing Linda Chalker-Scott's skepticism) but report real-world benefits
- See [[compost-tea]] and [[vermicomposting]]
