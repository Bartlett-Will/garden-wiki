# Episode 135: Running One of the Largest Living Soil Facilities in the US

> **Grower Interview** | [[adam-arnold|Adam Arnold]] of Collective Elevation, with [[tad-hussey|Tad Hussey]]

## Summary

[[tad-hussey|Tad Hussey]] interviews [[adam-arnold|Adam Arnold]], founder of Collective Elevation in Bozeman, Montana — one of the largest living-soil cannabis greenhouse operations in the country. Unlike the science-guest episodes, this is a practitioner's deep dive into the *operations* of growing at scale: how to control a greenhouse climate across a 150°F seasonal swing, how deep living-soil beds buffer environmental stress, how to scale a facility without sacrificing quality, and how to build the team and systems that hold it all together. Adam's background in conservation biology and wilderness management grounds the whole operation in sustainability.

## Key Takeaways

- **Living soil is a buffer.** Deep living-soil beds let plants tolerate poor VPD, high heat, and low humidity that would wreck a hydroponic crop — a simple living-soil hoop house can match a fully-automated greenhouse.
- **Stage the cheapest tool for the job.** Greenhouse control runs vents → small fans → exhaust fans → evaporative wet wall, with lockouts so heaters and exhaust never fight each other.
- **Wet walls are climate-specific.** Evaporative cooling shines in hot, dry Montana (30°F-plus differential) and is useless in humid regions.
- **Even, gentle under-canopy airflow** (VAF mushroom pattern, HAF racetrack, or low-velocity duct socks) eliminates the microclimates that breed mildew and bud rot.
- **Living soil out-produces CO₂ burners**, and dosing CO₂ when light is the limiting factor is pointless and harmful — a direct application of the Law of the Minimum.
- **Build out slowly.** Every jump in scale creates unforeseeable new problems; $8M handed over up front would have meant *more* mistakes, not fewer.
- **Trial everything small first.** A separate R&D/quarantine facility tests every light, genetic, amendment, and product before it reaches the main farm — and prevents "cure worse than the disease" disasters.
- **Use the sun.** Greenhouses slash power costs (lights off 90% of summer) and can improve quality versus sealed indoor rooms.
- **Systems and people scale the operation.** EOS accountability charts, stated vision and core values, teachable "blank-slate" hires, and living, season-specific SOPs.
- **"The eye" is earned.** Diagnostic intuition can't be put in an SOP — it comes from thousands of hours of [[field-observation|observation]].
