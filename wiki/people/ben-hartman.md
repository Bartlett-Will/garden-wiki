# Ben Hartman

> **Quick take:** A small-scale farmer who proved that less than one acre can generate over $100,000 per year by ruthlessly eliminating waste using lean manufacturing principles adapted from Toyota.

## Credentials

- Owner and operator of **Clay Bottom Farm**, Goshen, Indiana
- Farms on **less than one acre**, generating **$100,000+ per year** in revenue
- Named to **Grist 50** list of emerging green leaders
- Author of two books:
  - *The Lean Farm* — applying Toyota Production System principles to small-scale agriculture
  - *The Lean Farm Guide to Growing Vegetables* — crop-by-crop lean techniques
- Offers a **Masterclass** on lean farming methods

## Key Expertise

- [[lean-farming|Lean farming]] — adapting the Toyota Production System (5S, Kaizen, single piece flow) to agriculture
- [[the-5s-system|The 5S system]] — Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain applied to farm operations
- [[single-piece-flow-and-efficiency|Single piece flow]] — completing all steps on one item before moving to the next, consistently 2x faster than batch processing
- Small-scale high-value farming — maximizing profit per square foot rather than scaling acreage
- Waste identification and elimination — the seven types of Muda (waste) as they appear on a farm

## Notable Contributions and Claims

Ben Hartman's core insight is that most farms (and most businesses) are full of unrecognized waste. By systematically identifying and removing non-value-adding activities, small operations can become highly profitable without growing larger.

Key points:

- **Single piece flow nearly doubled his profits** — by bringing washing and packaging to the field and processing each item completely before setting it down, he reduced handling from 7 touches to 3-4.
- **Distributed tool placement** beats centralized tool sheds — duplicate any tool under $50 and place it at the point of use. "Tools in their place or in your hands."
- **The Five Whys** technique reaches root causes that surface-level fixes miss. Ben tells the story of a greenhouse-on-roof project where asking "why" five times revealed the actual problem.
- **Customer value comes first** — get orders before buying seeds, map every crop to a buyer. Owner fascination with a particular crop or technique is one of the biggest sources of waste.
- **Annual 5% expense reduction** compounds dramatically — trimming 5% per year from the ledger results in roughly 50% reduction over a decade.
- **"Get big or get out" is a myth** — Earl Butz's famous directive does not apply to small-scale direct-market farming.
